Some ideas do not fit a package. An internal knowledge assistant, a focused business tool, or a new way to coordinate work may need its own design. We start by understanding the users, the information involved, and the outcome you want to improve.

01

Find the useful first version

Identify who will use the tool, what they need to do, and what success would look like. Choose a focused starting point that can be evaluated.

02

Check feasibility early

Review data access, system connections, operational constraints, and the decisions that need human involvement before committing to the build.

03

Build in clear phases

Agree on milestones, deliverables, testing, and ownership. Scope ongoing support separately so you know what happens after launch.

A little more clarity

Questions, answered.

Can we start with an idea rather than a specification?+

Yes. Bring the problem, the people affected, and examples of the current process. Discovery helps turn the idea into an assessable scope.

Do you guarantee an idea is feasible?+

No. Feasibility depends on the systems, information, and requirements involved. We assess those before proposing a build.

Who maintains the project after launch?+

Handoff and support arrangements are agreed in the proposal. Ongoing managed support is scoped separately.
