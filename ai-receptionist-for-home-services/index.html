Collect the details dispatch needs

For a plumbing or HVAC inquiry, the useful handoff may include the caller’s name, callback number, address, and description of the issue. We agree which details matter and how they reach the person responsible for follow-up.

Separate an inquiry from a confirmed appointment

A request should not become a promise your crew cannot meet. We define what the front desk can say about availability, pricing, service coverage, and next steps. Scheduling connections are scoped around your actual systems.

Agree on urgent-call handling

Urgency rules and team escalation are part of the call-flow discussion. The front desk is not an emergency service. Your team defines which situations require a human handoff and which instructions are approved for the caller.

Questions before you start

Does this work for plumbing and HVAC companies?

Those are examples of home-service teams the front desk can be scoped for. We review your services, phone routing, and intake needs before agreeing on a build.

Can it book jobs into my dispatch software?

Booking and dispatch connections depend on your software, available APIs, and rules. We confirm feasibility and scope before promising a connection.

Does it give repair or safety advice?

The proposed flow focuses on intake and agreed next steps. Technical or safety-sensitive questions need boundaries and a human escalation plan agreed with your team.
