How to choose your first business automation

Pick a repeatable process with a clear owner, reliable inputs, and a result your team can check.

THE USEFUL TAKEAWAY

The best first project is a small, well-understood process with a visible handoff.

Look for repetition with a clear finish

List the tasks your team repeats during an ordinary week. A useful candidate has a recognizable trigger and an observable outcome: a website inquiry becomes an assigned task, for example. Avoid starting with “automate the whole office.” That hides the decisions, dependencies, and exceptions you need to understand.

Map one real example

Take a recent request and write down every step from arrival to completion. Record the tools used, information copied, person responsible, and points where the work waits. Ask the people doing the task to check the map. If different staff describe different processes, agree on the intended process before building around it.

Check whether the inputs are dependable

A workflow cannot route an inquiry by service type if the service field is usually missing. Identify the required fields, where they come from, and what should happen when they are incomplete. Simple form improvements or a clearer checklist may solve part of the problem before any automation is needed.

Keep an owner for exceptions

Choose who receives an alert when a connection fails or a request does not fit the rules. Define how staff can complete the work manually. For a first project, favor a task where someone can easily inspect the result and correct a mistake before it affects a customer.

Measure the same thing before and after

Record a practical baseline: time spent copying a request, number of incomplete handoffs, or time until a task is assigned. After launch, review comparable examples. Treat any projected savings as a hypothesis until you have measured the actual result. Your next-step checklist

  • Clear trigger and completion point
  • Required fields available
  • One accountable owner
  • Manual path when the connection fails
  • Baseline that can be measured again
